# Bounded trajectory witness V1

## Status and decision

This document locks the post-R1 design for
`bounded_trajectory_witness_v1`. W1 and the minimum W2 conformance checkpoint
now implement its typed artifacts and exact verifier; this is not a proposal
engine, an empirical capability result, or an integration decision.

V1 answers one narrow, controller-neutral question:

> For one valid `RouteCapabilityInputV1`, does there exist one finite canonical
> command sequence that the authoritative discrete plant can replay from the
> exact initial state through every required source and waypoint boundary within
> the fixed horizon?

`supported` means that one exact singleton trajectory has been replayed and
verified. It does not claim a continuous-time solution, a neighborhood of
initial or handoff states, robustness to perturbation, controller compatibility,
or production safety. Failure to find or verify a witness is `unknown`; V1 has
no physical `unsupported` decision.

The locked development plant runs at `120 Hz`. Commands are issued at `60 Hz`,
held for exactly two physics transitions, and bounded by `130 s`: at most
`15,600` physics steps and `7,800` command slots. These values are
behavior-bearing configuration, not features added to
`RouteCapabilityInputV1`.

This checkpoint adds the exact verifier and synthetic conformance tests, but no
solver, external dependency, maintained evidence fixture or run, planner
ranking change, controller change, or runtime screen. Existing R1 candidate
replay remains the exact frozen-executor oracle. D2 remains blocked.

V1 accepts a valid planner-V1 waypoint route with one or two emitted waypoints.
A valid direct route returns `unknown/scope/direct_route`; target flight after
the final emitted handoff and landing remain out of scope. Exact verification
does not use the empirical D1 training hull: either `horizontal_sign` and any
otherwise valid physical values in `RouteCapabilityInputV1` may be verified.
A proposal engine may declare a narrower domain and abstain outside it. That
model-level soundness is not permission to claim empirical coverage or to
expand the maintained corpus.

## Claim algebra

The physical prediction lane has two decisions:

- `supported`: a well-formed witness passes the exact verifier under the sealed
  input and witness configuration;
- `unknown`: no verified witness is available under the declared proposal and
  verification attempt.

Malformed input or artifacts, digest disagreement, and nondeterministic replay
are artifact status `invalid`, not a physical decision. There is no
`unsupported` variant or reason namespace in this schema. In particular, none
of these facts is a negative feasibility certificate:

- a finite enumeration reaches its budget;
- a convex relaxation reports infeasible;
- an optimizer fails to converge or reaches a local minimum;
- reconstruction, rounding, or exact replay rejects a proposed trace; or
- every proposal generated by one configured search is rejected.

Those outcomes map to `unknown`. A future negative reachability certificate
would need a separately reviewed schema, proof system, and design gate.

## Input and configuration boundary

The physical input is the existing canonical `RouteCapabilityInputV1`. Its
feature payload remains unchanged: resolved gravity and terrain, physical pad
geometry, vehicle, initial state, planning policy and safety profile, shaped
route geometry, and ordered physical waypoint contracts. Input provenance and
digests remain join/audit data and may not drive the witness generator.

`SimConfig`, controller identity or configuration, seed, mission outcome, D0/R1
evidence, route-family labels, planner rank, and prior comparison results remain
forbidden input features. V1 restores only the plant/cadence/horizon facts
needed to state the proof as typed, digest-covered witness configuration.

The proof-bearing payload of `BoundedTrajectoryWitnessConfigurationV1`
contains the following fields. An implementation may add provenance-only
metadata, but it may not add behavior-bearing fields without a schema review
and version bump.

| Group | Locked contents |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Schema ID `bounded_trajectory_witness_configuration_v1`; plant, command, phase/extractor, terrain/contact, and canonical-number semantic IDs; and configuration digest. Each semantic ID changes when its referenced behavior changes. |
| Cadence and horizon | `physics_hz = 120`, `command_hz = 60`, hold ratio `2`, `max_time_s = 130`, `max_physics_steps = 15_600`, and `max_command_slots = 7_800`. Validation recomputes the integer relationships rather than trusting redundant values. |
| Dynamics | The exact `pd-core` discrete update/order version, fuel and minimum-throttle mapping rule, mass rule, attitude slew/shortest-angle rule, semi-implicit translation rule, and command admissibility rules. Physical constants and vehicle values remain in the input. |
| Boundaries | Source progress/crossing rule, waypoint opportunity and sticky-window rule, first-contract-pass rule, phase ordering, final termination rule, and the exact comparison semantics used by the repository types. The neutral rules run at every physics state, independently of the production mission evaluator's command-cadence checks. |
| Contact | Rotated touchdown-point and rotated hull-vertex sampling rule, piecewise-linear terrain sampling rule, contact-classification ordering, and initial source-contact exception. |
| Verification | Maximum replay work `15_600` steps, two same-process verification repetitions, required recomputation/digests, and stable rejection/invalid reason mapping. No adjustable physical clearance or robustness margin is introduced. |

The configuration digest covers every behavior-bearing field above. It does not
cover a run ID, case ID, output path, timestamp, human label, solver log, or
other provenance-only value.

A proposal engine has a separate
`BoundedTrajectoryProposalConfigurationV1`. It declares its engine/version,
parameterization, numerical settings, iteration/search limits, reconstruction
rule, determinism settings, and proposal-configuration digest. A prediction
that reports `unknown` because proposal work ended is bound to that configuration
and budget. A verified witness remains independently checkable without trusting
the proposal engine.

## Exact command witness

`BoundedTrajectoryWitnessV1` contains:

- schema and witness digests;
- input and witness-configuration digests;
- one ordered list of `BoundedTrajectoryCommandV1` values;
- the claimed terminal physics step;
- no sampled states, controller markers, outcome labels, or claimed verifier
  result that the verifier would have to trust.

Proposal identity and logs do not belong in the witness. The enclosing physical
prediction may join an optional proposal-configuration digest and audit
provenance without changing the independently verifiable witness bytes.

Each command contains its zero-based slot and canonical finite
`throttle_frac` and `target_attitude_rad`. Slots are contiguous from zero.
Throttle is already in `[0, 1]`; the verifier must reject rather than silently
clamp an out-of-range value. Attitude targets are restricted to the closed
interval `[-std::f64::consts::PI, std::f64::consts::PI]`. Exact `-pi` and
`+pi` endpoints are both retained and are
behaviorally distinct: the current shortest-angle rule preserves that tie's
sign and therefore slews in opposite directions from zero. Other periodically
equivalent encodings are not valid witness bytes. Negative zero is forbidden
for both fields; a proposal canonicalizes it to positive zero before sealing,
and the verifier invalidates a noncanonical witness.

The canonical trace begins with the physical step-0 state and
`Command::idle()` as a pre-install anchor. Slot zero is then installed at step
zero before transition `0 -> 1`. For `k > 0`, slot `k` is installed at physics
step `2k` only after the post-step state, contact/neutral-boundary decisions,
and terminal checks for step `2k` have been recorded, and before transition
`2k -> 2k + 1`. It remains held through transition
`2k + 1 -> 2k + 2`.

Thus a post-step state records the command that produced it; at an even state
`2k > 0`, that is slot `k - 1`, matching `run_simulation` sampling before its
next controller update. The trajectory stream separately records each command
installation event at its step. A witness terminating at post-step state `n`
contains exactly `ceil(n / 2)` command slots. An odd terminal step uses the
last command for one transition; there is no implicit or ignored command tail.
The terminal step must be in `1..=15_600`.

The plant applies each held command exactly as the authoritative simulator does:

1. slew attitude toward the target by at most the vehicle rotation rate for one
   physics step, using shortest-angle displacement;
2. map zero throttle to zero thrust and positive throttle through the input
   vehicle's minimum-throttle fraction, then consume the available fuel;
3. compute thrust using the post-burn fuel/mass state and updated attitude;
4. update velocity and then position for the `1 / 120 s` step;
5. advance step/time and update extrema;
6. evaluate authoritative contact, then the verifier's physics-rate neutral
   source/route contracts, then the maximum-time boundary. Contact wins when a
   contact classification and final handoff occur on the same post-step state.

The current development inputs carry `min_throttle_frac = 0.25`, but the proof
binds the vehicle value in the input and the mapping rule in the configuration;
it does not hard-code a second vehicle model.

## Exact verifier

`BoundedTrajectoryVerificationV1` is produced only by a deterministic exact
verifier. Its artifact status is `complete` or `invalid`; a complete result has
verdict `verified` or `rejected`. A rejected candidate is a valid failed proof
attempt and causes a physical prediction of `unknown`, not `invalid` and not
`unsupported`.

Structural/schema/digest failure, a noncanonical command, or nonfinite input
makes the verification artifact `invalid` with no verdict. `rejected` is
reserved for a structurally valid command trace whose exact physical replay
contacts terrain, misses or fails an ordered boundary, exceeds the horizon, or
does not reach the claimed terminal boundary at the claimed step. If the final
handoff occurs earlier than claimed, the nonminimal trace is also `rejected`;
the canonical witness stops at the first final pass.

The verifier must:

1. validate and digest the complete `RouteCapabilityInputV1`, witness
   configuration, and witness before replay;
2. reconstruct the authoritative initial singleton and exact route context;
3. invalidate noncanonical, nonfinite, out-of-range, missing, duplicate, extra,
   or misnumbered commands rather than repairing them;
4. replay every physics transition in order with the locked command schedule;
5. apply the authoritative discrete contact semantics to every post-step state;
6. derive source and waypoint boundaries only from physical state and immutable
   route contracts;
7. stop successfully at the first post-step state that satisfies the final
   required handoff, including at step `15,600` when progress evaluation wins
   the simulator's declared ordering; and
8. recompute the canonical trajectory, boundary, and verification digests on
   every load. The enclosing prediction validator separately recomputes its
   prediction digest and all cross-artifact joins.

The verifier does not accept a mission goal or mission outcome and does not use
the production mission evaluator's `60 Hz` checkpoint cadence. It applies the
already-locked D0 neutral rules to every `120 Hz` post-step state. A future
implementation must not invoke the whole mission runner in a way that lets its
terminal state or cadence override this neutral proof boundary.

Verification uses the repository's `f64` operations and exact comparison rules.
V1 adds no solver tolerance, clearance reserve, or near-boundary robustness
test to the physical claim. A state exactly accepted by the frozen repository
rule is accepted. Nonfinite arithmetic or disagreement between repeated exact
replays is `invalid/numerical/*` or `invalid/determinism/*`; it is not converted
to a physical negative.

The canonical trajectory digest covers the pre-install step-0 state with its
idle command, every ordered command-install event, and every post-step
proof-bearing state. State material includes position, velocity, attitude,
angular rate, fuel/mass, the command that produced that state, contact
classification, ordered phase state, and any boundary/contract resolution.
Persisting every full state is optional because it can be recomputed, but the
verification artifact preserves the terminal state, extrema, and every
decisive raw boundary state/bracket.

### Contact and terrain authority

The verifier uses the simulator's rotated touchdown points and rotated
rectangular hull vertices, sampling the authoritative piecewise-linear terrain
at those world-space vertex positions. It follows the current discrete
post-step contact classification and penetration-tolerance ordering exactly.
The initial source-pad contact state is the allowed anchor; a classified contact
on a later state before final handoff rejects the witness.

The planner's axis-aligned `exact_point_clearance`, corridor envelope, and
centerline geometry remain useful to a proposal engine and for diagnostics.
They are not substitutes for the proof verifier's physical contact rule. V1
also makes no claim about an unsampled collision between two physics states or
terrain contact along hull edges beyond the simulator's vertex-sampling model.

V1 deliberately adds no intermediate route-leg centerline, corridor-envelope,
or tracking-error acceptance constraint. Apart from the source transition, a
route leg is accepted by exact contact-free plant evolution into the next
ordered waypoint contract. The shaped centerline and planner envelope may guide
proposal generation and remain digest-bound input geometry, but they do not
silently become a second flight contract.

W1 exposes a narrow `pd-core` verification surface for one authoritative plant
step and exact post-step contact classification rather than copying private
simulation math into `pd-eval`. The ordinary simulator keeps contact outcome,
mission progress, and timeout ownership. Differential tests cover the shared
plant path without changing runtime behavior.

### Ordered phase and handoff authority

Phase results use the existing `RouteCapabilityPhaseV1` identities. Boundary
names are terminal events of those phases rather than additional enum variants:

```text
InitialState
  -> PadDeparture [terminal boundary: contact_exit]
  -> Acquisition [terminal boundary: tracking_entry]
  -> RouteLeg { leg_index: 0 }
  -> Handoff { waypoint_index: 0 } [terminal boundary: first contract pass]
  -> ...
  -> RouteLeg { leg_index: n }
  -> Handoff { waypoint_index: n } [terminal boundary: final contract pass]
```

There is no target leg, terminal descent, or landing phase. `contact_exit` and
`tracking_entry` use the D0 physics-rate directed adjacent-pair crossing rule.
Both raw endpoint states are authoritative; interpolation is diagnostic only.
The first qualifying crossing is retained, and later backtracking/re-entry does
not select a replacement.

Each waypoint uses the existing neutral D0 opportunity semantics:

- first capture-window entry is derived from adjacent physics states, with an
  initially-inside state represented explicitly;
- first directed deadline-plane crossing is retained. If the first searched
  state is already on or beyond the deadline plane, it is
  `initial_deadline`: opportunity begins there, the contract is evaluated once
  with `window_seen` set only when that state is also inside the capture radius,
  and the handoff resolves immediately without a synthetic bracket;
- `window_seen` becomes sticky after any raw state inside the capture radius;
- `TransferWaypointSpec::assess_handoff` is evaluated at each physics-rate raw
  state from the first opportunity;
- the first contract-passing state before or at the deadline is authoritative;
  and
- controller phases, handoff markers, private guidance predictions, and mission
  outcome labels are never consulted.

A verified trajectory must complete the source phases and every waypoint in
order without an earlier contact terminal. Later evidence cannot repair an
earlier unresolved phase. The terminal proof state may be any physics-rate
post-step state; it need not coincide with a command boundary.

## Artifacts and stable reasons

The intended typed boundary is:

| Artifact | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `BoundedTrajectoryWitnessConfigurationV1` | Freezes exact plant, cadence, horizon, command, phase, contact, numeric, and verifier semantics. |
| `BoundedTrajectoryProposalConfigurationV1` | Freezes one untrusted proposal/search engine and its budgets; absent when a witness is supplied directly. |
| `BoundedTrajectoryCommandV1` | Carries one canonical command slot. |
| `BoundedTrajectoryWitnessV1` | Carries the exact finite command sequence and proof identities. |
| `BoundedTrajectoryVerificationV1` | Records exact replay verdict, phase/boundary results, terminal state, extrema, recomputed trajectory digest, and first reason. |
| `BoundedTrajectoryWitnessPredictionV1` | Seals one `supported` or `unknown` physical decision and joins the input/configuration, proposal, witness, and verification identities. |
| `PhysicalExecutorComparisonV1` | Reports physical-witness and frozen-executor axes without changing either result. |

These artifacts use dedicated closed enums:

- `BoundedTrajectoryArtifactStatusV1::{Complete, Invalid}`;
- `BoundedTrajectoryDecisionV1::{Supported, Unknown}`; and
- `BoundedTrajectoryVerificationVerdictV1::{Verified, Rejected}`.

They must not alias or reuse `CapabilityDecision` or
`RouteExecutionPredictionV1`, because those D1 types admit `Unsupported` and
have different proof semantics. They also use a dedicated
`validate_bounded_trajectory_reason_v1` validator rather than the current D1
`validate_reason_code` function.

`BoundedTrajectoryWitnessPredictionV1` has artifact status `complete` or
`invalid`. Only a complete artifact has a decision and first reason. A
`supported` decision requires exactly one witness identity and one matching
`verified` verification identity. An `unknown` decision has no verified
witness; it may retain ordered rejected-verification identities as diagnostics,
and it must carry the proposal-configuration identity when its reason depends
on proposal work or budget. An invalid prediction has no decision. Its digest
covers schema, input/configuration joins, status/decision/reason, optional
proposal configuration, ordered attempted-witness/verification identities, and
the supported witness identity when present.

Stable physical-prediction reasons begin with:

- `supported/exact_witness_verified`;
- `unknown/coverage/bounded_search_exhausted`;
- `unknown/coverage/no_verified_witness`;
- `unknown/numerical/proposal_nonconverged`;
- `unknown/numerical/proposal_reconstruction_rejected`; and
- `unknown/scope/*` for a valid input outside the locked V1 scope.

Verification rejection reasons identify the first contact, source phase, route
leg, handoff, terminal-step, or horizon boundary that rejects the trace. They are
diagnostic under the dedicated `rejected/*` namespace; they do not create an
`unsupported/*` physical namespace. Malformed/tampered input or artifacts use
the witness validator's `invalid/input/*`, `invalid/artifact/*`,
`invalid/numerical/*`, and `invalid/determinism/*` families. The validator also
accepts only the `supported/*`, `unknown/scope/*`, `unknown/coverage/*`, and
`unknown/numerical/*` prediction families listed above. Reasons remain
nonempty ASCII path-like strings, and the first decisive reason in proof order
is authoritative.

Canonical serialization and digest construction follow repository practice:
schema/version and every proof-bearing ordered field are covered; a digest
never covers itself; map/set order is canonical; no nonfinite number is
serialized; and provenance-only locations, timestamps, labels, and logs stay
outside behavior identity. V1 reuses `pd-eval`'s existing compact
`serde_json` canonical bytes, declaration-ordered structs, `BTreeMap` ordering,
and FNV-1a canonical digest rather than introducing a second digest algorithm.
Loading any artifact reruns schema, finite-value, cross-reference, and digest
validation.

## Physical and executor axes stay separate

`bounded_trajectory_witness_v1` and R1 originate from different procedures. A
comparison joins a pre-run sealed physical-witness prediction to a later or
separately sealed frozen-executor result; it may not feed the executor's action
trace or decision back into witness proposal.

| Sealed physical witness prediction | Frozen executor | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `supported` | `supported` | One exact physical trace exists and the frozen executor also completes its exact pair. No robustness claim. |
| `supported` | `unsupported` | Physical/executor gap for that input and executor. This is not a physical false accept. |
| `supported` | `unknown` | Physical existence is witnessed; executor compatibility is unresolved. |
| `unknown` | `supported` | The pre-run proposal was incomplete. The later executor trace is post-seal exact-pair evidence of existence, but does not retroactively change the prediction. |
| `unknown` | `unsupported` | Both questions remain physically unresolved. Executor failure is not infeasibility. |
| `unknown` | `unknown` | Both lanes abstain. |

An invalid artifact on either axis invalidates only the joined comparison until
repaired; it is not a decision on the other axis. Therefore the old D1
development rule “supported prediction joined to scoped executor failure is a
false accept” does not apply to this existential witness lane. A future gate
must score the two axes explicitly.

One `PhysicalExecutorComparisonV1` joins one exact route/input pair. Validation
recomputes the candidate's `RouteCapabilityInputV1` and requires its physical
input digest and route-plan digest to match both source artifacts. It also binds
the base-row/candidate identity and frozen executor-pairing digest as join/audit
provenance. A selected-route physical result may not be joined to an
alternative-candidate executor replay, even if their human labels match. The
comparison digest covers both source-artifact digests, the exact join keys, both
unchanged axis results/reasons, comparison status, and interpretation category.
None of those provenance fields may change either source decision.

An executor-supported action trace can be submitted to the V1 exact verifier
afterward. If it verifies, it is a valid post-run singleton witness under the
matching physical configuration. That audit is recorded separately and cannot
be scored as a pre-run proposal success or replace the already sealed physical
prediction. For W4, physical artifacts must be generated and sealed from inputs
alone before existing R1 actions, evidence, or decisions are opened for the
join; otherwise the comparison is circular.

The three R1 selection-gap cases are already-seen descriptive requirements for
this comparison. They must not tune proposal parameters, planner ranking, or
acceptance rules after their outcomes are visible.

## Ownership and implementation sequence

Ownership remains narrow:

- `pd-eval` owns research DTOs, proposal orchestration, exact-verification
  artifacts, sealing, comparisons, and invalidation accounting;
- `pd-core` owns the authoritative plant, neutral waypoint contracts, terrain,
  and any narrow pure step/contact surface needed to avoid duplicated physics;
- `pd-plan` is unchanged and R1 exposure remains research-only;
- `pd-control` is neither an input nor a dependency of the physical witness;
- `pd-report` may display sealed results later but is not an authority.

Implementation remains split into reviewable gates:

1. **W1 — types and exact verifier (complete):** typed configuration, witness,
   verification, and physical-prediction artifacts now live in `pd-eval`; the
   minimum neutral `pd-core` step/contact surface provides exact parity. No
   proposal solver was added.
2. **W2 — verifier conformance (minimum checkpoint complete):** synthetic
   one/two-waypoint feasible traces, rejected contact/deadline/nonminimal
   traces, cadence and horizon edges, D0 boundary semantics, mutation/tamper
   tests, canonical round-trips, repeat determinism, and differential parity
   against the authoritative simulation path cover the initial conformance
   matrix. Additional cases may be added without weakening this gate.
3. **W3 — proposal backend decision/spike:** compare a finite predeclared search
   or sequential-convex proposal path under reproducible local dependencies.
   The engine proposes only; exact replay owns every positive claim. No spike
   output is a negative certificate.
4. **W4 — development comparison:** on already-seen development inputs only,
   seal the physical lane, then join it with R1/frozen-executor results using
   `PhysicalExecutorComparisonV1`. Do not change planner ranking or maintained
   fixtures.
5. **Later evidence gate:** only after W1-W4 and a fresh design review may the
   project define a new exposure registry, held-out corpus, advancement
   thresholds, or any D2 work.

W1 and the minimum W2 conformance checkpoint are implemented and validated.
Backend/library choice, trajectory parameterization, warm starts,
convexification details, and search budgets remain deferred to W3 because they
do not alter the soundness boundary: a proposal becomes `supported` only after
exact replay.

## Acceptance gates

The W1/W2 checkpoint is complete only when:

- ordinary planner, controller, simulator, and maintained-fixture behavior is
  unchanged;
- the exact verifier is differentially identical to the authoritative plant for
  command schedule, state evolution, and contact, and identical to the D0
  neutral extractors for physics-rate ordered phase decisions;
- every supported artifact reloads, replays, and reproduces canonical bytes and
  digests;
- hand-authored feasible witnesses verify, while command mutation, collision,
  missed/deadline contracts, horizon overflow, nonfinite data, and digest
  tampering cannot produce `supported`;
- rejected well-formed proposals map to `unknown`, with no physical
  `unsupported` path in the V1 API;
- no controller value, recorded outcome, R1 result, route label, seed, or
  provenance alias enters generation or verification math; and
- workspace tests, strict linting, formatting, and documentation checks pass
  with zero invalid artifacts.

Passing W1/W2 establishes a trustworthy positive-certificate mechanism, not a
useful proposal algorithm. D2, production integration, and any negative
feasibility claim remain blocked until separately designed and reviewed.
